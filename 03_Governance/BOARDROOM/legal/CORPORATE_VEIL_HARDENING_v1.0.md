## CORE PRINCIPLE

**Absolute Separation.**

- Business money never touches personal money
- Personal guarantees are never signed
- Personal assets are never commingled with corporate assets
- Personal credit is never used as corporate collateral

**If you cannot explain a transaction without saying "it's both personal and business," it fails this test.**

---

## PART 1: BANKING SEPARATION (Non-Negotiable)

### Rule 1.1: Income Flow

**ALL business income flows into LLC accounts only.**

- Revenue from any source → LLC bank account
- Payments from customers/vendors → LLC account
- Loan proceeds → LLC account
- Founder investment → LLC account (as formal capital contribution)

**Never**:
- Deposit business income into personal account
- Have customers pay personal credit card
- Have customers pay Founder personally
- Commingle revenue streams

**Violation consequence**: Veil pierce. Creditor can claim Founder's personal assets.

---

### Rule 1.2: Expense Flow

**ALL business expenses paid from LLC accounts only.**

- Vendor payments → LLC bank account or LLC card
- Payroll → LLC bank account
- Equipment → LLC card
- Software → LLC card
- Insurance → LLC bank account

**Never**:
- Pay business expenses from personal account
- Use personal credit card for business
- Pay LLC obligations from personal funds (even temporarily)
- Reimburse yourself before documenting

**Violation consequence**: IRS audit + veil pierce + personal liability.

---

### Rule 1.3: Founder Compensation

**Founder only receives money through formal, documented channels:**

**Option A: Salary (Recommended)**
- Defined amount per pay period
- Documented in annual minutes or operating agreement
- Paid via LLC payroll (or transfer with written record)
- Reported on tax return

**Option B: Distributions**
- Approved by written resolution
- Documented in LLC minutes
- Transferred via bank transfer (not cash)
- Recorded in operating agreement
- Only if LLC has profits

**Option C: Reimbursement**
- Founder pays business expense personally
- Submits receipt to LLC
- LLC reimburses via check/transfer
- Documented in accounting records

**Never**:
- Founder "borrows" from LLC without documentation
- Founder takes cash distributions
- Founder uses LLC card for personal purchases then reimburses
- Informal draws

**Violation consequence**: LLC is deemed personal piggy bank. Veil pierced. Assets at risk.

---

### Rule 1.4: Personal Purchases (Absolute Prohibition)

**ZERO personal purchases on LLC cards or accounts.**

This is non-negotiable.

**Never**:
- Groceries on LLC card
- Netflix on LLC card
- Gas (personal use) on LLC card
- Phone bill (personal) on LLC card
- Rent (personal residence) on LLC card
- Any personal expense, ever

**Even if you pay it back immediately, this violates the veil.**

**Why**: Creditors will argue LLC is merely an extension of personal finances. Court agrees. Founder liable.

---

### Rule 1.5: Monthly Reconciliation

**Every month, Founder or accountant must:**

1. Review LLC bank statements
2. Review LLC credit card statements
3. Check for any personal charges
4. Check for any commingling
5. Reconcile to business purpose
6. Document in accounting system

**Red flags that trigger immediate escalation to Erebus:**
- Unexplained transfers
- Personal vendor charges
- Round-number "draws" (suggests personal use)
- Gaps in documentation
- LLC account balance below operating minimum

## PART 2: DOCUMENTATION RULES (Audit-Proof)

### Rule 2.1: Receipt & Purpose Documentation

**Every expense >$25 requires:**

1. Receipt (saved, dated, from vendor)
2. Business purpose (2-3 sentences in accounting system)
3. Relevant category (supplies, equipment, services, etc.)
4. Date transaction posted

**Example (correct)**:
```
Date: 2025-01-15
Vendor: AWS
Amount: $127.43
Purpose: Monthly cloud hosting for Sentinel City digital infrastructure
Category: Technology/Hosting
Receipt: Filed in QuickBooks
Approver: Founder
```

**Example (incorrect - veil risk)**:
```
Date: 2025-01-15
Vendor: Amazon
Amount: $47.99
Purpose: ???
Category: Miscellaneous
Receipt: Lost
Approver: ???
```

**Violation consequence**: IRS challenge + veil pierce (court says "commingling is evident").

---

### Rule 2.2: Formal Compensation Resolution

**Every time Founder takes money from LLC, there must be documentation:**

**Annual Resolution (in LLC minutes):**
```
RESOLUTION: FY2025 FOUNDER COMPENSATION

Be it resolved that Sentinel Labs LLC authorizes the following founder compensation:

Salary: [amount] per [pay period]
OR
Distributions: [amount] per [frequency]

Effective: [date]
Reason: For services rendered / Return on capital investment
Approved by: Founder (sole member)
Documented: [date of minutes]
```

**Bank transfer should reference the resolution:**
```
Reference line: "FY2025 Founder Compensation Resolution - [date]"
```

**Keep this forever.**

---

### Rule 2.3: Operating Agreement (Current & Signed)

**Massachusetts LLC Requirement:**

An operating agreement is legally required (or implied by statute).

**Yours must include:**

1. **Member**: [Founder name]
2. **Capital Contribution**: [amount invested]
3. **Compensation**: Founder salary or distribution formula
4. **Distributions**: How profits are paid out
5. **Meetings**: How decisions are documented
6. **Dissolution**: What happens if LLC ends

**Must be:**
- Dated
- Signed by Founder
- Current (updated if compensation changes)
- Kept in LLC records
- Referenced in annual minutes

**Violation consequence**: Court says no formal agreement = personal liability.

---

### Rule 2.4: Annual Minutes (Even Single-Member)

**Massachusetts law suggests (strongly) that even single-member LLCs document major decisions.**

**Annual minutes must include:**

1. **Fiscal year reviewed**: [dates]
2. **Compensation approved**: Salary/distributions for next year
3. **Major decisions**: Any significant business decisions
4. **Bank account status**: Accounts in good standing

**Format**:
```

Annual Member Meeting
Date: [date]

Member Present: [Founder name]

1. FY2025 Founder Compensation (see resolution above)

3. Bank accounts operational and properly separated from personal accounts

APPROVED BY: [Founder signature]
DATE: [date]

```

**Keep all minutes forever.**

## PART 3: COMMINGLING DEATH LIST (Instant Veil Pierce)

These actions **immediately pierce the veil**:

### ❌ Paying Personal Bills from LLC Account

If a court sees:
- LLC account paying Founder's mortgage
- LLC account paying Founder's car payment
- LLC account paying Founder's insurance

**Court conclusion**: "LLC is personal piggy bank. No veil. Seize all assets."

---

### ❌ Depositing LLC Income into Personal Account

If a court sees:
- Customer payment deposited to personal account
- Loan proceeds in personal account
- Revenue mixed with personal money

**Court conclusion**: "No separation. Business is personal. Founder liable."

---

### ❌ Using LLC Card for Personal Purchases

Even small amounts:
- Groceries on LLC card (even paid back)
- Gas for personal car on LLC card
- Netflix on LLC card

**Court conclusion**: "Cards are interchangeable. Founder treats business as personal."

---

### ❌ Personal Guarantees on LLC Debt

**Never sign a personal guarantee on:**
- LLC credit cards
- LLC loans
- LLC lease obligations
- LLC vendor contracts

**Why**: Personal guarantee explicitly tells the creditor "you can sue my personal assets."

**Exception**: Some early-stage lenders (banks, traditional financing) may require it. If forced:
1. Get written approval from Erebus first
2. Document that LLC couldn't qualify without it
3. Create plan to pay off the personal guarantee within 24 months
4. Revisit quarterly with Erebus

---

### ❌ Informal "Loans" to Founder

Never:
- "Borrow" $500 from LLC without documentation
- Take cash distributions
- Pay personal expense from LLC "temporarily"

**What happens**: Months later, IRS or creditor sees unexplained cash movement. They assume fraud. Veil pierces.

---

### ❌ Mixing Personal & Business Assets

Never:
- Put LLC money into personal investment account
- Use personal credit line to fund LLC
- Have personal and business use the same vendor account
- Share email addresses or legal documents

2. Update within 30 days
3. Update LLC records
4. Notify Erebus immediately

---

### Rule 4.2: Registered Agent

**Massachusetts requires an LLC to have a registered agent (person or service).**

**Options:**

**Option A: Registered Agent Service (Recommended)**
- Third-party service (e.g., LegalZoom, Northwest, Rocket Lawyer)
- Cost: $100–$300/year
- Handles legal document service
- More professional

**Option B: Founder as Agent**
- If Founder is registered agent
- Must maintain Massachusetts address
- Personally receives legal documents
- More vulnerable

**Recommendation**: Use a registered agent service. Keeps Founder personal address off public record.

## PART 5: ANNUAL COMPLIANCE (Massachusetts-Specific)

### Rule 5.1: Annual Report to Secretary of State

**Massachusetts requires every LLC to file an Annual Report:**

**Filing deadline**: Follows formation anniversary (typically June 30 each year)

**Report includes**:
- LLC name
- Registered office address
- Registered agent information
- Principal office address
- Manager/member names

**Cost**: ~$125/year

**If not filed**:
- LLC status becomes "administratively dissolved"
- Veil protection is lost
- Personal liability reinstated
- Penalties apply

**Action**: File on time, every year. Mark calendar.

---

### Rule 5.2: Beneficial Ownership Report (BOIR)

**Federal requirement (as of 2024):**

If Sentinel Labs is a reporting company, file:
- **FinCEN Beneficial Ownership Report**
- Lists: Founder name, address, DOB, ID number
- **Deadline**: First year: within 90 days of formation
- **Subsequent**: Within 30 days of any change

**Check FinCEN website for current requirements.**

---

### Rule 5.3: Tax Filings

**Federal and Massachusetts tax returns must be filed separately:**

**Federal (IRS)**:
- If taxed as S-Corp: Form 1120-S
- If taxed as C-Corp: Form 1120
- If taxed as sole proprietorship (default): Founder's Form 1040 with Schedule C

**Massachusetts (Massachusetts Department of Revenue)**:
- File separate return, not combined with personal return
- Use LLC EIN, not Founder's SSN

**Timing**: Typically April 15 for prior fiscal year

**If tax filings show commingling**: Veil pierce + penalties + audit.

## PART 7: WHAT HAPPENS IF VEIL PIERCES

### Scenario: Creditor Sues

**If a creditor sues Sentinel Labs LLC and wins, they can seek:**

- All LLC assets
- All Founder personal assets (if veil pierced)
- Founder's home
- Founder's savings
- Founder's future wages (garnishment)
- Founder's business ownership

**How court decides if veil is pierced:**

1. **Was there commingling?** (If yes, veil weak)
2. **Was business separated from personal?** (If no, veil weak)
3. **Were formalities followed?** (Minutes, resolutions, agreements)
4. **Was personal guarantee signed?** (If yes, automatic liability)
5. **Did Founder treat LLC as personal?** (Evidence of piercing)

**If Erebus has documented clean separation: Veil holds. Founder protected.**

**If Erebus found commingling: Veil failed. Founder exposed.**
