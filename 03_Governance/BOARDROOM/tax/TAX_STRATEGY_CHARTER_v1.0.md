## PART 1: ENTITY ELECTION STRATEGY

### Rule 1.1: Default Election (Disregarded Entity)

**Sentinel Labs is structured as:**

- **Business type**: Single-member LLC (Massachusetts)
- **Federal tax classification**: Disregarded entity
- **What this means**: 
  - LLC itself pays no federal income tax
  - All income/expenses flow through to Founder's personal 1040
  - Founder files Schedule C (self-employment) with personal return
  - Simpler than separate entity, cheaper than S-Corp

**Why this structure**:
- Minimal IRS attention (single-member disregarded LLCs are common)
- No separate tax return to audit
- Lower compliance burden
- Perfect for early-stage companies <$100k revenue

**Form filed with IRS**: None required (default election is automatic)

---

### Rule 1.2: S-Corp Election (Trigger: First Payroll)

**The moment Founder starts taking formal payroll (not just distributions):**

**File Form 2553 (S-Corp election) with the IRS:**

- **Timeline**: File within 2 months of first payroll OR before April 15 of following tax year
- **Cost**: $0 (included in tax return)
- **Reason**: S-Corp allows salary + distributions, reducing self-employment tax

**Example**:
```
If payroll starts in March 2026:
- File Form 2553 by May 31, 2026
- OR by April 15, 2027
- Takes effect immediately
```

**Once S-Corp election is filed**:
- Sentinel Labs files Form 1120-S (separate return)
- Founder files K-1 (capital account statement)
- Self-employment tax drops from ~15% to ~2.9% (Medicare only on salary portion)

**Critical**: This decision requires **Erebus pre-approval** (see Part 7).

---

### Rule 1.3: Never C-Corp (Hard Rule)

**Do NOT elect C-Corp status unless**:
- Revenue exceeds $1,000,000+ annually
- Founder has specific tax strategy reasons
- Erebus explicitly approves

**Why not C-Corp**:
- Double taxation (corporate + personal)
- More complex compliance
- Larger audit surface
- Annual Form 1120 (higher audit probability)

**C-Corp only makes sense at scale.**

## PART 2: FILING CALENDAR (Non-Negotiable)

### Rule 2.1: Federal Tax Return

**Disregarded Entity (Current)**

**Filing deadline**: April 15 (following tax year)

**What to file**:
- Founder's personal Form 1040
- Schedule C (Self-Employment Income)
- Schedule SE (Self-Employment Tax)
- Itemize Sentinel Labs income/expenses in Schedule C

**Preparation**:
- Accountant or CPA prepares return
- Founder reviews and approves
- File electronically (e-file) before April 15

**Example timeline for 2025 taxes**:
- Tax year ends: December 31, 2025
- Return due: April 15, 2026
- Deadline to file: April 15, 2026 (no extensions without reason)

**If Form 2553 (S-Corp) is filed**:
- File Form 1120-S instead of Schedule C
- Due date: March 15 (earlier than 1040)
- Founder still files 1040, but attaches K-1

---

### Rule 2.2: Massachusetts Annual Report

**Massachusetts Secretary of State requires annual report:**

**Filing deadline**: Anniversary of LLC formation + 30 days

**Example for Sentinel Labs LLC (formed November 10, 2025)**:
- Annual Report due: December 10, 2025 (or by June 30 if filed by then)
- Then every year on anniversary

**What to include**:
- LLC name
- Registered office address
- Registered agent name
- Manager/member names
- Principal office address

**Cost**: ~$125/year (check current SOS fee)

**If not filed**:
- LLC becomes "administratively dissolved"
- Veil protection is lost
- All liability falls on Founder personally
- Penalties apply

**Action**: File 30 days before anniversary. Set calendar reminder now.

---

### Rule 2.3: Estimated Tax Payments (If Self-Employed)

**If Sentinel Labs income is expected to exceed $400 (net profit):**

**File quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES):**

**Payment schedule**:
- **Q1 (Jan-Mar)**: Due April 15
- **Q2 (Apr-Jun)**: Due June 15
- **Q3 (Jul-Sep)**: Due September 15
- **Q4 (Oct-Dec)**: Due January 15 (following year)

**How much to pay**:
- Estimate annual self-employment tax + income tax liability
- Divide by 4
- Pay each quarter

**Example**:
```
Estimated 2025 income: $60,000
Estimated self-employment tax (15.3%): ~$8,500
Estimated income tax (22% bracket): ~$13,200
Total annual: ~$21,700
Quarterly payment: ~$5,425
```

**Penalty for underpayment**: 8% annual interest on late payments

**If S-Corp election is filed**:
- Estimated payments change (salary vs. distribution split)
- Accountant recalculates quarterly
- Usually lower due to reduced self-employment tax

---

### Rule 2.4: Sales Tax (If Applicable)

**If Sentinel Labs sells physical products or certain services in Massachusetts:**

**Register for sales tax:**
- File with Massachusetts Department of Revenue
- Obtain sales tax registration number

**Filing frequency**:
- Monthly or quarterly (depends on volume)
- File via MassTaxConnect (online portal)

**Current Massachusetts sales tax rate**: 6.25%

**Example**:
```
If selling a $100 product:
- Collect $6.25 sales tax
- File monthly return showing collections
- Remit $6.25 to state
```

**If Sentinel Labs is service-only (software, consulting)**:
- Sales tax may not apply
- Consult accountant or check SOS guidance
- File application for exemption if applicable

## PART 3: DEDUCTION IRON RULES (Audit-Proof)

### Rule 3.1: Every Expense >$25 Needs Documentation

**For every business expense >$25:**

1. **Receipt** (physical or digital)
   - Date
   - Vendor name
   - Amount
   - What was purchased

2. **Business purpose** (2-3 sentences)
   - Why was this purchased?
   - Who will use it?
   - What business function does it serve?

3. **Category** (for accounting)
   - Office supplies
   - Technology/software
   - Meals (if allowed)
   - Travel
   - Professional services
   - Other

4. **Timestamp** (when recorded)
   - Date expense was recorded
   - Who approved it

**Example (audit-proof)**:
```
Date: January 15, 2026
Vendor: AWS
Amount: $127.43
Category: Technology/Cloud Hosting
Purpose: Monthly cloud infrastructure costs for Sentinel City digital environment
Receipt: Filed in QuickBooks - Doc ID 2026-001-127
Approver: Founder
```

**Example (audit red flag)**:
```
Date: January 15, 2026
Vendor: Amazon
Amount: $47.99
Category: ???
Purpose: (blank)
Receipt: (lost)
Approver: (unknown)
```

**IRS will challenge the second one immediately.**

---

### Rule 3.2: Home Office Deduction (Simplified Method Only)

**If Founder works from home, deduction is allowed:**

**DO NOT use actual expense method (audit risk).**

**Use simplified method**:
- $5 per square foot of home office space (up to 300 sq ft)
- Maximum deduction: $1,500/year
- No receipts needed
- Just measure the room and claim deduction

**Example**:
```
Home office size: 200 square feet
Simplified deduction: 200 × $5 = $1,000
Claim on Schedule C, no documentation needed
```

**Why simplified method**:
- IRS expects this for small businesses
- No detailed expense records required
- Audit probability is near-zero
- Easy to defend if questioned

**Do NOT claim**:
- Actual rent/mortgage
- Utilities
- Home insurance
- Property tax allocation

**IRS will audit this immediately and disallow it.**

---

### Rule 3.3: Meals (50% Deduction Rule)

**If Sentinel Labs has business meals:**

**Only 50% is deductible.**

**Example**:
```
Business meal with vendor: $50
Deductible amount: $50 × 50% = $25
```

**What qualifies**:
- Working meals with business purpose
- Client entertainment
- Team lunch during work meeting

**What does NOT qualify**:
- Founder's personal lunch (not business purpose)
- Groceries
- Home meals
- Daily coffee

**Documentation required**:
- Receipt showing vendor, date, amount
- Note of attendees and business purpose

**Example (audit-proof)**:
```
Date: February 1, 2026
Vendor: Restaurant ABC
Amount: $75
Attendees: Founder + Finance Director
Purpose: Quarterly budget planning meeting
Deductible (50%): $37.50
```

---

### Rule 3.4: Hard Rule: No Personal Expenses Ever

**This is absolute.**

Never code personal expenses as business:
- Personal groceries (not deductible)
- Personal utilities (not deductible)
- Personal rent (not deductible)
- Personal vehicle (unless actual business miles tracked)
- Personal phone (unless % business use documented)

**If an expense is mixed personal/business**:
- Calculate % that is legitimate business
- Deduct only that percentage
- Document the allocation

**Example (correct)**:
```
Phone bill: $100/month
% business use: 40% (calls with vendors, emails, etc.)
Deductible: $100 × 40% = $40
Documentation: Log of business calls, emails, etc.
```

**Example (audit disaster)**:
```
Phone bill: $100/month
Deductible: $100 (claiming 100% as business)
Result: IRS challenge, potential audit, disallowance
```

## PART 4: RECORDKEEPING (Erebus Enforcement)

### Rule 4.1: Dedicated Tax Records Folder

**Maintain organized records:**

**Physical or digital system**:
- All receipts saved
- Organized by month/category
- Cross-referenced to accounting software
- Easy for Erebus or accountant to review

**Structure example**:
```
Tax Records 2026/
├── January/
│   ├── AWS_receipt_127.43.pdf
│   ├── Office_supplies_45.22.pdf
│   └── [etc.]
├── February/
│   ├── [receipts]
├── [etc. by month]
```

**Retention**: Keep all records for **7 years** (IRS audit statute of limitations)

---

### Rule 4.2: Monthly Bank Reconciliation

**Every month, Founder or accountant must:**

1. Download bank statements from LLC account
2. Cross-check to QuickBooks/accounting software
3. Ensure all transactions are categorized
4. Flag any missing receipts
5. Verify no personal charges

**Timeline**: Complete by 15th of following month

**Issues identified**:
- Missing receipts → add documentation or reclassify
- Personal charges → investigate immediately
- Unexplained transfers → document or reverse

---

### Rule 4.3: Quarterly Erebus Tax Review

**Every quarter (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct), Erebus reviews:**

1. **Accounting software** (QuickBooks or equivalent)
   - Are all income items recorded?
   - Are all expenses properly categorized?
   - Are deductions audit-proof?

2. **Bank statements**
   - Any commingling?
   - Any personal charges?
   - Any suspicious transfers?

3. **Receipt file**
   - Are receipts organized?
   - Are all major expenses documented?
   - Are business purposes clear?

4. **Estimated payment status**
   - Is estimated tax current?
   - If payroll, are withholdings correct?

**Erebus action on finding**:

**No issues**: Green light. Continue as planned.

**Documentation gap** (minor): Request receipt/purpose within 30 days.

**Tax risk** (moderate): Halt that category of expense, escalate to Founder.

**Veil/audit risk** (critical): Red lantern. Founder meeting required.

## PART 5: OWNER DRAWS (Labeled Transfers Only)

### Rule 5.1: How Founder Gets Paid

**Before S-Corp election:**

**Option A: Owner Draw** (recommended)
- Founder transfers money from LLC to personal account
- **Must be labeled**: "Owner Draw - [month/date]"
- **Must be documented**: Annual resolution approved in minutes
- **Frequency**: Monthly, quarterly, or annual (consistent)

**Example (correct)**:
```
Date: January 31, 2026
Amount: $5,000
From: Sentinel Labs LLC bank account
To: Founder personal account
Label: "January 2026 Owner Draw"
Documented in: Operating Agreement section X
Approved: Annual resolution FY2026
```

**Example (audit disaster)**:
```
Date: January 31, 2026
Amount: $5,000
From: Sentinel Labs LLC bank account
To: Founder personal account
Label: (blank or "transfer")
Documented in: (nothing)
```

**Option B: Distribution** (if LLC has profits)
- Only if LLC is profitable
- Documented in operating agreement
- Approval resolution required
- Quarterly/annual frequency

**Option C: Reimbursement** (if Founder paid personal for business)
- Founder submits receipt
- LLC reimburses via transfer
- Must be documented
- Usually within 30 days

**Option D: Salary** (only after S-Corp election)
- Formal payroll set up
- W-2 issued to Founder
- Payroll taxes withheld
- Only after Form 2553 filed

---

### Rule 5.2: Never Informal Draws

**DO NOT**:
- Take cash from LLC account without documentation
- "Borrow" from LLC without formal approval
- Pay personal bills directly from LLC account
- Treat LLC account as personal piggy bank

**All draws must be documented in writing and labeled.**

## PART 6: RED LINES (Instant Audit Triggers)

These actions immediately flag the LLC for IRS audit:

### ❌ Paying Personal Taxes from LLC Account

Never use LLC funds to pay:
- Founder's personal income tax
- Founder's self-employment tax
- Founder's capital gains tax
- Founder's property tax

**IRS flag**: Commingling. Audit opened immediately.

---

### ❌ Missing Estimated Payments

If estimated payments are required and missed:

- **Penalty**: 8% annual interest on unpaid amount
- **Audit trigger**: Missing payments signal unreliability
- **Compounding**: Interest accrues each quarter

---

### ❌ Commingling (Any Form)

- LLC income deposited to personal account
- Personal expenses paid from LLC account
- LLC card used for personal purchases
- Personal guarantees on LLC debt

**IRS conclusion**: Business is personal. Audit all accounts.

---

### ❌ Disorganized Records

- Missing receipts for expenses >$100
- No business purpose documented
- No reconciliation between bank and accounting software
- Handwritten ledger (no software backup)

**IRS conclusion**: Lack of record-keeping suggests fraud. Audit opened.

---

### ❌ Late Filing

- Federal return filed after April 15 (no extension filed)
- Massachusetts Annual Report filed after deadline
- Estimated payments made late

**Penalties**: 5% per month of unpaid tax + interest
