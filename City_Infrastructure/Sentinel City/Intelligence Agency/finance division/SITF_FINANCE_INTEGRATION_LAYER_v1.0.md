# **1. Purpose**

The SITF–Finance Integration Layer establishes a unified intelligence membrane between:

* **Financial operations** (banking, credit-building, compliance, payments, vendor activity)
  and
* **Visibility operations** (ripple mapping, propagation modeling, discovery throttling).

Its purpose is to transform every financial action into an **analyzable, predictable, and controllable visibility event**.

This layer ensures:

1. **No financial step is taken without visibility forecasting.**
2. **No external system wakes up unless Founder intends it.**
3. **Every signal generated by finance becomes SITF-grade intelligence.**

This integration is the backbone of Ghost Mode.

# **3. Core Directives**

### **3.1. Directive Alpha — Visibility Before Action**

Before any financial action is executed, SITF must issue a visibility forecast stating:

* Which external systems will be woken
* In what order
* Over what time range
* With what threat level
* And what ripples will propagate from the event

No action occurs until the Founder sees this forecast.

---

### **3.2. Directive Beta — One Signal at a Time**

Only one major visibility-emitting financial action may be executed at once.

This allows SITF to observe:

* Ripple propagation
* System latency
* Downstream activation
* Cross-domain dependencies

This is the “slow-move chess” doctrine of Ghost Mode.

---

### **3.3. Directive Gamma — Dormancy Preservation**

The Finance Department must preserve system dormancy wherever beneficial.

Dormancy is defined as:

> **An external system that could be aware of Sentinel Labs but is not yet activated.**

Examples of dormant systems:

* IRS (until EIN is created or used)
* SBA (until EIN is used)
* Credit bureaus (until DUNS+EIN pair exists)
* National banks (until EIN + bank activity match)
* FinCEN (until KYC triggers)

Dormancy is an asset.
Dormancy is power.
Dormancy must not be broken accidentally.

---

### **3.4. Directive Delta — SITF Ownership of Ripples**

All financial visibility events (bank letters, credit offers, IRS correspondence, vendor mail) must be logged in:

* `SITF_PROPAGATION_MAP_v1.0.md`
* `VISIBILITY_RIPPLES_REGISTRY_v1.0.md`

Finance does not archive mail; SITF does.

Finance executes.
SITF interprets.

---

### **3.5. Directive Epsilon — Founder Review Gate**

Certain actions cannot occur without explicit Founder authorization:

* EIN creation
* First bank account opening
* Any credit applications
* DUNS pairing with EIN
* Any action that wakes a Tier-1 surveillance domain

These are classified as **Sovereign Activation Events**.

# **4. Visibility Tiers for Financial Actions**

The SITF Finance Integration Layer classifies all financial actions into five visibility tiers.

---

## **Tier 0 — Silent Actions (No External Visibility)**

**Examples:**

* Internal bookkeeping
* Drafting financial plans
* Updating internal spreadsheets
* Internal vendor research
* Building credit strategy (no execution)

**Threat Level:** None
**SITF Posture:** No Review Required

---

## **Tier 1 — Noise Layer (Low-Value, Non-Invasive Visibility)**

**Examples:**

* Receiving unsolicited EIN spam
* Receiving marketing mail
* Receiving general commercial solicitations
* Using home mailing address
* Not yet having EIN or bank accounts

**Threat Level:** Low
**SITF Posture:** Log and Observe (no intervention)

---

## **Tier 2 — Structural Visibility (State-Level Awareness)**

**Examples:**

* LLC formation
* State registry updates
* Registered agent filings

**Domains Activated:**

* State → USPS → low-tier data brokers

**Threat Level:** Moderate
**SITF Posture:** Log, Measure, Forecast

---

## **Tier 3 — Federal Activation (High Visibility)**

**Examples:**

* Obtaining an EIN
* Filing IRS forms
* Opening business bank account
* KYC-level identity checks
* Merchant processing setup
* DUNS + EIN pairing

**Domains Activated:**

* IRS
* FinCEN
* OFAC
* SBA
* Full commercial data broker chain
* National banks

**Threat Level:** High
**SITF Posture:** Requires Founder Authorization + Full Visibility Forecast

---

## **Tier 4 — Credit Surface Awakening (Very High Visibility)**

**Examples:**

* Vendor trade lines (reporting)
* Credit card usage
* Credit limit increases
* Net-30/60/90 accounts
* Bank transaction patterns

**Domains Activated:**

* D&B
* Experian Business
* Equifax Business
* Automated underwriting models
* Lending algorithms

**Threat Level:** Very High
**SITF Posture:** Precisely Timed, One-At-A-Time Execution

---

## **Tier 5 — National Exposure (Maximum Visibility)**

**Examples:**

* Applying for business loans
* Attempting SBA acquisition financing
* Applying for high-limit credit lines
* Requesting bank underwriting review

**Domains Activated:**

* National lending models
* Fraud detection grids
* Secondary market data pools
* Industry-wide risk models

**Threat Level:** Critical
**SITF Posture:** Major Foundational Decision — “Do Not Rush”

# **5. Cross-System Activation Map**

This section details how a single financial action wakes multiple external systems simultaneously.

```
Action: EIN Creation
Triggers: IRS → SBA → Credit bureaus → Banking watchlists → Data brokers

Action: Bank Account Opening
Triggers: FinCEN → OFAC → Bank KYC → Transaction monitoring models

Action: DUNS Registration
Triggers: D&B → Experian → Equifax → Vendor verification grids

Action: Vendor Net-Terms Account
Triggers: Credit bureaus → D&B payment reporting → Underwriting systems
```

Each of these must be pre-modeled by SITF before execution.

The Founder maintains a **Visibility Budget** — a limit on how many high-visibility signals may be emitted per quarter.

* **Tier 3 actions:** 1 per quarter
* **Tier 4 actions:** 2 per quarter

SITF enforces this budget.

This prevents accidental overexposure and maintains Ghost Mode.
