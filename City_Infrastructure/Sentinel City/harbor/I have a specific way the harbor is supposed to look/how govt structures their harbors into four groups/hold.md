Governments structure their harbors in roughly **four standard models** (plus a few hybrids).  
Here’s the real-world playbook, 2025 edition:

| Model | Who owns the land & infrastructure | Who runs day-to-day operations | Who collects the money | Real-world examples | % of global ports |
|-------|------------------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| 1. Landlord Port (most common) | Government (national/port authority) owns land, quays, breakwaters | Private terminal operators (DP World, PSA, COSCO, APM Terminals) lease for 20–99 years | Government gets land rent + royalties; operators keep cargo fees | Rotterdam, Singapore, Los Angeles, Antwerp, Hamburg, Dubai | ~70 % |
| 2. Fully Public Port | Central or local government owns **and** operates everything | Civil-service port authority staff | All revenue goes straight to government treasury | Most Chinese ports (Shanghai, Ningbo), many U.S. military ports, Cuba, North Korea | ~20 % |
| 3. Fully Privatized Port | Private company owns land + infrastructure outright | Same private owner | Private company keeps everything | Felixstowe (UK), some small UK trust ports sold off in 1980s–90s | <5 % |
| 4. Tool Port / Service Port (legacy) | Government owns everything | Government does stevedoring too (very rare now) | Government | Some very small African, Pacific island ports | <5 % |

### Hybrid flavors you see everywhere

| Hybrid | Description | Examples |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Public-Private Partnership (PPP) | Government builds, private runs under concession | Lisbon, Piraeus (COSCO 67 % stake) |
| Corporatized Public Authority | Government owns 100 %, but runs as commercial company | Port of New York/New Jersey, Vancouver, Sydney |
| Municipal Port | City owns and runs (often small) | Boston, San Francisco (small terminals) |

### U.S. Specific (you asked about Massachusetts)

| Port | Ownership | Operator | Notes |
|------|-----------|----------|-------|
| Boston (Conley Terminal) | Massport (state authority) | Private leases (e.g., Maersk) | Landlord model |
| New Bedford/Fall River | Mass state + municipal | Mostly private fish terminals | Hybrid |
| Gloucester | City owns some piers | Private fishing co-ops | Municipal |
| Provincetown | Town owns pier | Private ferry companies | Classic small municipal |

### Bottom line (2025)

- **Landlord model wins** because governments keep the real estate (which only goes up in value) and let private operators compete on efficiency.  
- **Pure public ports** are shrinking outside China.  
- **Pure privatization** almost never happens anymore (too politically toxic).

So when you look at a busy harbor today, 9 times out of 10 the land and breakwaters are government-owned, and the cranes and terminal gates are run by a multinational on a 30–50-year lease.

That’s the global standard.
